01 / 07
What the test measures
“
The test measures the lowest and highest notes you would let another person hear — today, in this room, with the voice you warmed up or did not.
Floor
Lowest
An audible note you can reproduce.
Ceiling
Highest
An audible note you can reproduce.

The result records today’s edges. Potential and future change sit outside the measurement.

02 / 07
Make the next test comparable
“
Honest means the same conditions every time, and the same definition of a note every time.
01
Warm up
Use the same short set. Avoid testing cold or after hard singing.
02
Match setup
Keep the room, time of day, and phone distance steady.
03
Count a note
Require clear pitch, no strain, and enough stability for two beats.
04
Stop honestly
End the test when effort replaces height.
03 / 07
The note rule
“
A note counts if it has an identifiable pitch, arrives without strain, and could be held for a beat or two.
Counts
Settles and holdsThe pitch stays clear for two beats.
Does not count
Creaks or breaksA sound at the edge never becomes a usable note.
04 / 07
When the result looks wrong
“
A disappointing result usually has a boring explanation. Work through these before you believe it.
Cold voice
Retest after the warmup you normally use.
Different hour
Morning and evening can produce different edges.
Pushing
Extra effort can narrow the top instead of extending it.
Room or phone
Noise, distance, and placement can hide quiet notes.
Rushed edge
Give the final notes time to settle.
Two plausible causes? Discard the reading and test another day.
05 / 07
Read the label loosely
“
What it is not is an identity, a ceiling, or a verdict.
The label sees
Your lowest note and your highest note on one test.
The label misses
Tessitura, timbre, transition points, and sustained load.

Use it as a closest fit for finding a starting key, not as permission for what to sing.
