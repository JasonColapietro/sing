## In short

Vibrato is a small, regular swing in pitch around the note's center, with three dials: rate, how many swings a second; extent, how far above and below the center it moves; and onset, when it starts, at once or after a stretch of straight tone. Regularity separates vibrato from an unsteady note. The Vibrato hold warm-up reports rate and extent after each hold; nothing times the onset, so that dial is yours to judge, and the easiest to practice first.

## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose Vibrato hold. Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading A vocabulary for tone is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Piano notes supply the pitches; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Choose a comfortable sustained note and establish three easy seconds of straight tone before adding any oscillation.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Vibrato control

Vibrato has three parameters. Controlling them is what separates it from a wobble. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: Keep the supplied reference or reading visible, settle your posture, and use only a pitch and duration you can repeat comfortably.
- Listen: The vibrato begins and ends on cue and sounds even rather than becoming a wide uncontrolled wobble.

### Hear the distinction

On Vibrato hold, sing two long holds on the same comfortable note, changing only the onset: in the first, stay straight for about one second before you let the vibrato in; in the second, stay straight for about three, which still leaves the vibrato time to settle before the hold ends. Record both in one take in the recorder with a breath between them, and on playback listen for where the wobble starts in each. If vibrato does not come on its own, a straight note is a fine answer. Invite an even vibrato rather than shaking the jaw or pulsing the abdomen. Return deliberately to straight tone and release.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If oscillation appears only through shaking or pressure, return to straight tone, shorten the hold, and work with a teacher before adding speed.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.
