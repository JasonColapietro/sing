## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose Vibrato hold. Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading A vocabulary for tone is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Synthesized pitches supply the notes; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Choose a comfortable sustained note and establish three easy seconds of straight tone before adding any oscillation.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Vibrato control

Heard and held, not scored. The vibrato drill in warm-ups reads rate and width, but nothing times vibrato on cue, so the switch itself is your ear and your recording. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: Keep the supplied reference or reading visible, settle your posture, and use only a pitch and duration you can repeat comfortably.
- Listen: The vibrato begins and ends on cue and sounds even rather than becoming a wide uncontrolled wobble.

### Make one evidence take

State the checkpoint target, record one uninterrupted attempt in the [recorder](/recorder), and keep the result even when it does not pass. Before singing, choose the count where the vibrato will come in and the count where you will return to straight tone, for example in on three and out on six of a slow count. Invite an even vibrato rather than shaking the jaw or pulsing the abdomen. Return deliberately to straight tone and release.

- Look: Keep the supplied reference or reading visible, settle your posture, and use only a pitch and duration you can repeat comfortably.
- Listen: The vibrato begins and ends on cue and sounds even rather than becoming a wide uncontrolled wobble.

### Review your own attempt

I compared straight tone with naturally occurring vibrato, without shaking the jaw or manufacturing a wobble. Compare your attempt with the supplied material and choose one smaller adjustment for next time.

- Look: Note the pitch, key, or setup you used so that your next comparison is useful.
- Listen: Listen for the specific change taught in this lesson. A self-check is your own reflection, not an automatic vocal score.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If oscillation appears only through shaking or pressure, return to straight tone, shorten the hold, and work with a teacher before adding speed.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.

## Practice blocks

- 45s: Open the practice material. Prepare the room, body, and safe baseline for “Self-Check: Straight to Vibrato on Cue.”
- 90s: Isolate the vibrato control target in short, comfortable examples.
- 135s: Practice or record the complete “Self-Check: Straight to Vibrato on Cue” task, resetting between attempts.
- 90s: Review one take against the stated listening goal and write the next smaller correction.

- I compared straight tone with naturally occurring vibrato, without shaking the jaw or manufacturing a wobble.
- The vibrato begins and ends on cue and sounds even rather than becoming a wide uncontrolled wobble.
- I kept missing or uncertain evidence as missing instead of awarding myself a measured pass.

Ready when: On two different days, in three holds in a row, you start straight, let the vibrato in at the count you chose before singing, and return to straight tone before the release, with no jaw or belly shake.

If not ready: If oscillation appears only through shaking or pressure, return to straight tone, shorten the hold, work with a teacher before adding speed, and repeat a smaller version on another fresh attempt rather than forcing the checkpoint. If no vibrato comes on its own yet, that is not a fault to fix here: keep the holds straight, steady and easy, move on to the next module, and come back to this one in a few weeks rather than shaking one in.

What this shows: I compared straight tone with naturally occurring vibrato, without shaking the jaw or manufacturing a wobble. This is a self-report, not an automatic assessment.

What this does not show: An automatic pitch, rhythm, register, tone, or safety result. Vibrato onset time, switching on cue, or healthy production. The vibrato drill in warm-ups reads rate and width after a hold, but this lesson does not score them.
