## In short

An ornament that is easy in the middle of your voice gets harder near the edges, because the edges already take most of your control. That is the difficulty of a phrase with a wide leap and an ornament: each is manageable alone, and they compete when they arrive together. Choose a key where both ends are repeatable, rehearse the leap and the ornament separately, and keep the top lighter, not louder, when you join them.

## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose Deep River or Amazing Grace (Opening Lines, Verses 1 & 2). Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading Transposition without shame is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Piano notes supply the pitches; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Once you have taken the range test, song practice opens the study fitted to your measured range; without a saved range it opens in its written key and is scored in either octave. Shift it a semitone at a time until both ends are repeatable, then mark each ornament and the phrase that crosses the widest interval.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Extended-range repertoire

Range and agility at the same time, which is the actual difficulty. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: The key keeps the melody inside your comfortable range, every ornament has a written location, and no phrase depends on a last-second reach.
- Listen: The full take keeps the authored melody recognizable and the planned ornaments audible.

### Hear the distinction

Take the phrase you marked as crossing the widest interval and sing it twice in the same key: first plain, then with one small scoop or fall on the note at the top of the leap. Change only the ornament. Record both in one take in the recorder with a breath between them, and on playback check that the ornamented version still lands the top note as cleanly as the plain one. Rehearse the range, ornament, and continuity demands separately before combining them. Keep the top lighter rather than louder.

- Look: The key keeps the melody inside your comfortable range, every ornament has a written location, and no phrase depends on a last-second reach.
- Listen: The full take keeps the authored melody recognizable and the planned ornaments audible.

- Look: Note the pitch, key, or setup you used so that your next comparison is useful.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If ornaments disappear at range edges, remove them, secure the phrase, then restore one at a time. Change key if an endpoint is forced.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.

## Practice blocks

- 30s: Open the practice material. Prepare the room, body, and safe baseline for “Range and Ornaments in a Short Phrase.”
- 60s: Isolate the extended-range repertoire target in short, comfortable examples.
- 90s: Practice or record the complete “Range and Ornaments in a Short Phrase” task, resetting between attempts.
- 60s: Review one take against the stated listening goal and write the next smaller correction.
