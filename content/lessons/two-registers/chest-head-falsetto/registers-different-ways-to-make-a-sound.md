## In short

A register is a way of setting up the vocal folds; each setup handles one band of pitches well and gives out above it. In chest voice, the setup you speak in, the folds are shorter and thicker and close firmly: a full, speech-like tone. In head voice they stretch longer and thinner but still close: lighter, and it can still ring. In falsetto they stretch without closing fully, so the sound is airy. No setup covers a whole range.

### Hear the distinction

Pick one comfortable note from Humming thirds, low enough that you could say a word on it. Sing it on “ah” twice in one take, with a quiet breath between: first fuller and speech-like, as if calling a word across a small room; then lighter, with less weight, like a gentle hoot. The setup is the only thing that changes, so match pitch and volume as closely as you can. Record your takes in the recorder and listen for a change in color and weight, not just in loudness.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If the second version only appears by getting louder or tighter, lower the pitch and volume and make the contrast smaller.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.
