## In short

Breathy, nasal and tight all get called a bad tone, but they are three different sounds. Breathy is a haze of air in the tone, usually from folds not meeting fully. Nasal is sound colored by the nose, though a pinched, bright tone often only seems nasal: pinch your nose on an “ah”; if the sound changes, some was going through the nose. Tight, or pressed, is more squeeze than the note needs. Name the sound before you change it.

## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose Tongue-trill descent or Soft sustain. Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading When the numbers lie to you is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Synthesized pitches supply the notes; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Choose a short, comfortable phrase at moderate volume. Record the relaxed baseline and one changed example in one take, with a quiet breath between them. Make only one deliberate change; record your takes in the recorder.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Breathiness, nasality, tension

Three different problems that get called the same thing. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: The pitch and words remain the same across comparisons; no version requires a raised chin, locked jaw, or sustained pressure.
- Listen: Name the audible difference without diagnosing its cause. Breathy, nasal, and tight are perceptions that can overlap.

### Hear the distinction

Sing one short, comfortable phrase at moderate volume as your baseline. Then sing it again changing one thing on purpose, gently and only as a listening demonstration: a little more air in the tone (breathy), more of the sound through the nose (nasal), or a slightly firmer, held sound (tight). Use only one of the three per take, keep the pitch, words and volume the same, and return to the easy baseline straight after. Record your takes in the recorder and, on playback, name the difference you hear without diagnosing its cause.

- Look: The pitch and words remain the same across comparisons; no version requires a raised chin, locked jaw, or sustained pressure.
- Listen: Name the audible difference without diagnosing its cause. Breathy, nasal, and tight are perceptions that can overlap.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If a contrast causes discomfort, hoarseness, loss of range, or effort that remains after the attempt, stop the exercise and rest your voice.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.
