## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose Tongue-trill descent or Soft sustain. Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading When the numbers lie to you is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Piano notes supply the pitches; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Choose a short, comfortable phrase at moderate volume. Record the relaxed baseline and one changed example in one take, with a quiet breath between them. Make only one deliberate change; record your takes in the recorder.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Breathiness, nasality, tension

Targeted release before targeted effort. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: The pitch and words remain the same across comparisons; no version requires a raised chin, locked jaw, or sustained pressure.
- Listen: Name the audible difference without diagnosing its cause. Breathy, nasal, and tight are perceptions that can overlap.

### Alternate attempt and reset

Work in short repetitions with a normal breath and complete release between them. Rest the tongue tip against the back of your lower front teeth. With one hand resting lightly on your chin, sing your baseline phrase on “yah-yah-yah”, letting the tongue make each “y” while the jaw stays still under your hand. Then sing it on “mah”, letting the jaw drop loosely on each “ah” without pushing it open. Record the release version straight after the baseline in one take and compare.

- Look: The pitch and words remain the same across comparisons; no version requires a raised chin, locked jaw, or sustained pressure.
- Listen: Name the audible difference without diagnosing its cause. Breathy, nasal, and tight are perceptions that can overlap.

### Review your own attempt

I compared a relaxed baseline with a later example in the same recording and described one audible change without forcing a rough sound. Compare your attempt with the supplied material and choose one smaller adjustment for next time.

- Look: Note the pitch, key, or setup you used so that your next comparison is useful.
- Listen: Listen for the specific change taught in this lesson. A self-check is your own reflection, not an automatic vocal score.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If a contrast causes discomfort, hoarseness, loss of range, effort that remains after the attempt or any blood, stop the exercise and rest your voice. Any blood, or a sudden loss of range or voice, means seeing a doctor who can examine the vocal folds, such as an ENT or laryngologist, now; anything still there after rest means the same.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.

## Practice blocks

- 45s: Open the practice material. Prepare the room, body, and safe baseline for “Release the Jaw and Tongue.”
- 90s: Isolate the breathiness, nasality, tension target in short, comfortable examples.
- 135s: Practice or record the complete “Release the Jaw and Tongue” task, resetting between attempts.
- 90s: Review one take against the stated listening goal and write the next smaller correction.
