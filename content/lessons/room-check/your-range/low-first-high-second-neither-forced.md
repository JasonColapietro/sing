## In short

The range test asks for your two ends one at a time: hold a comfortable note, slide down to your lowest, then up to your highest. Only notes you could let someone else hear count, held clearly for a beat or two; the creak below your lowest sung note and a squeezed top note stay out. Stop each slide where the next note would need effort rather than height: that reading repeats next month; an extreme reached once does not.

## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose First hum or Easy fourth siren. Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading Reading your range test is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Piano notes supply the pitches; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Warm up first, for about four minutes with the [Morning reset](/warmups?routine=morning): a cold voice under-reports at both ends. Then start near the middle of your comfortable speaking range. Keep the jaw loose and choose a small lip trill, hum, or easy vowel rather than a loud call.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Range scan and voice type

Comfortable, not extreme. The number we want is one you can repeat next month. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: Keep the supplied reference or reading visible, settle your posture, and use only a pitch and duration you can repeat comfortably.
- Listen: Both slides remain conversational in effort. The last usable note sounds repeatable, not like an extreme reached once.

### Hear the distinction

Record two slides down from the same comfortable note in one take in the recorder, with a reset between them. On the first, stop at the first note that needs any effort: extra volume, a tucked chin or a pushed breath. On the second, stop one note earlier, while it is still completely easy. The stopping note is the only thing that changes. On playback, listen for the difference: the effortful note usually thins, rattles or wobbles, and the easy one sounds like the rest of your voice, so keep the easy one as your low end. To put a name on it, slide down to that easy note once more in the [pitch studio](/studio), which shows the note as you sing it, and write down the note you stop on. Return to the middle and do the same pair going up, naming the easy top note the same way. Stop before squeezing, pain, scratchiness, or the urge to reach for a number.

- Look: Keep the supplied reference or reading visible, settle your posture, and use only a pitch and duration you can repeat comfortably.
- Listen: Both slides remain conversational in effort. The last usable note sounds repeatable, not like an extreme reached once.

### Review your own attempt

I slid down twice, stopping at the first note that needed effort and then one note earlier, did the same going up, and wrote down the easy low and high notes. Compare your attempt with the supplied material and choose one smaller adjustment for next time.

- Look: Note the pitch, key, or setup you used so that your next comparison is useful.
- Listen: Listen for the specific change taught in this lesson. A self-check is your own reflection, not an automatic vocal score.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If an endpoint needs extra volume, a lifted chin, or neck effort, discard it, return to the middle, and keep the last easy repeatable note.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.

## Practice blocks

- 30s: Open the practice material. Prepare the room, body, and safe baseline for “Low First, High Second, Neither Forced.”
- 45s: Isolate the range scan and voice type target in short, comfortable examples.
- 60s: Practice or record the complete “Low First, High Second, Neither Forced” task, resetting between attempts.
- 45s: Review one take against the stated listening goal and write the next smaller correction.
