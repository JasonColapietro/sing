## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose Legato triad or Wee from the fifth. Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading Registers, and why they exist is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Synthesized pitches supply the notes; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Work only after an easy warm-up, on a pitch below the top of your comfortable range. Keep attempts short and have a clear stop rule before starting.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Comfortable mixed-voice coordination

Hold six comfortable seconds, then report effort and stop on any warning sign. The app does not verify strain. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: The chin stays level, the jaw remains available, and the pitch does not require a raised chest or rigid neck.
- Listen: The tone stays clear and repeatable for the planned short hold, with an easy release and no rasp that persists afterward.

### Make one evidence take

Say “hey!” as if calling a friend across a quiet room — a call, not a shout. Sing that “hey” on a comfortable note below your transition and move it up one semitone at a time only while it stays as easy as speaking, to find the last easy step; if it starts to need a push, let it lighten instead. Then hear the pattern you chose, Legato triad or Wee from the fifth, in the key that puts its top note just above your transition but never above that last easy step, and stop the reference. State the checkpoint target and record one uninterrupted attempt in the [recorder](/recorder): the pattern three times in a row at moderate volume, holding its top note for six comfortable seconds each time, with a quiet breath between. Keep the result even when it does not pass. Never chase a belt label by adding throat pressure.

- Look: The chin stays level, the jaw remains available, and the pitch does not require a raised chest or rigid neck.
- Listen: The tone stays clear and repeatable for the planned short hold, with an easy release and no rasp that persists afterward.

### Review your own attempt

I tried the supplied short pattern gently, returned to an easy baseline, and noted my own effort. Compare your attempt with the supplied material and choose one smaller adjustment for next time.

- Look: Note the pitch, key, or setup you used so that your next comparison is useful.
- Listen: Listen for the specific change taught in this lesson. A self-check is your own reflection, not an automatic vocal score.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => Stop immediately for pain, burning, sudden hoarseness, loss of range, or effort that lingers. Resume another day or work with a qualified teacher; do not repeat to prove toughness.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.

## Practice blocks

- 45s: Open the practice material. Prepare the room, body, and safe baseline for “Self-Check: Easy Tone and Effort.”
- 75s: Isolate the comfortable mixed-voice coordination target in short, comfortable examples.
- 120s: Practice or record the complete “Self-Check: Easy Tone and Effort” task, resetting between attempts.
- 60s: Review one take against the stated listening goal and write the next smaller correction.

## Self-check

- I tried the supplied short pattern gently, returned to an easy baseline, and noted my own effort.
- The tone stays clear and repeatable for the planned short hold, with an easy release and no rasp that persists afterward.
- I kept missing or uncertain evidence as missing instead of awarding myself a measured pass.

Ready when: Three times in a row, the pattern crosses your transition at moderate volume with the same easy, call-like effort you have below it, you hold its top note for six comfortable seconds, and your speaking voice feels unchanged afterward.

If not ready: Stop immediately for pain, burning, sudden hoarseness, loss of range, or effort that lingers. Resume another day or work with a qualified teacher; do not repeat to prove toughness. When you come back, start from a smaller version, and only once your speaking voice feels normal.

What this shows: I tried the supplied short pattern gently, returned to an easy baseline, and noted my own effort. This is a self-report, not an automatic assessment.

What this does not show: An automatic pitch, rhythm, register, tone, or safety result. Safe belt, absence of strain, pressed-phonation status, tissue health, or medical readiness; the app cannot judge those.
