## In short

Around your transition, a strong harmonic of the note you are singing meets the main resonance of the vowel and then climbs past it, and the reinforcement drops out from under the sound. Shading the vowel a little toward the neutral middle moves that resonance so the crossing stays smooth: a bright “ee” opens slightly toward “ih”, an “oo” toward “oh”, and a wide “ah” rounds slightly toward “aw”. Use the smallest change that works; listeners rebuild a word from context generously, but if they can hear the word change, you have gone too far.

## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose Ng siren to the fifth or Gentle fifth siren. Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading Resonance, placement and the vowel is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Synthesized pitches supply the notes; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Choose a word whose vowel crosses your transition zone and sing it first below the zone at easy volume.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Vowel modification

Small shape adjustments that keep the crossing smooth and the lyric intelligible. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: The jaw and lips change gradually through the crossing; the tongue does not retract sharply and the word remains recognizable.
- Listen: The pitch remains connected and the lyric is still understandable across all three vowels.

### Hear the distinction

Pick a word with an “ah” in it, such as “far”, and sing it on a slide up a fifth, starting a little below your transition so the slide crosses it. Do it twice in one take: first keeping the vowel exactly as you speak it, then letting the “ah” round slightly toward “aw” as you climb. The vowel shape is the only thing that changes, so keep the pitch and the volume the same. On playback, listen for which crossing stays connected and whether the word is still clearly “far”. Record your takes in the recorder. Narrow or shade the vowel only enough to keep the crossing easy while preserving the word.

- Look: The jaw and lips change gradually through the crossing; the tongue does not retract sharply and the word remains recognizable.
- Listen: The pitch remains connected and the lyric is still understandable across all three vowels.

### Review your own attempt

I compared three vowels on a comfortable fifth slide and noted which small adjustment helped continuity. Compare your attempt with the supplied material and choose one smaller adjustment for next time.

- Look: Note the pitch, key, or setup you used so that your next comparison is useful.
- Listen: Listen for the specific change taught in this lesson. A self-check is your own reflection, not an automatic vocal score.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If the word disappears, reduce the modification. If the crossing tightens, return to a hum and rebuild the vowel from there.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.

## Practice blocks

- 30s: Open the practice material. Prepare the room, body, and safe baseline for “Change the Vowel, Keep the Word.”
- 60s: Isolate the vowel modification target in short, comfortable examples.
- 90s: Practice or record the complete “Change the Vowel, Keep the Word” task, resetting between attempts.
- 60s: Review one take against the stated listening goal and write the next smaller correction.
