## In short

Your passaggio is the two-to-four-note zone where the voice hands over from a thicker fold setup to a thinner one and the resonance that helped drops away, so notes need effort, thin out or crack. It often sits near your voice type's published zone, but can land a semitone or more either side and moves with vowel and volume. Contraltos, mezzos and sopranos often meet a lighter shift first, around E4 to G4. Find yours on a slow, quiet slide.

### Set a safe baseline

Warm up gently, then choose a narrow range that crosses a place where production tends to change. Use moderate volume and short attempts.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Locating your own transition zone

- Look: Keep the supplied reference or reading visible, settle your posture, and use only a pitch and duration you can repeat comfortably.
- Listen: You can anticipate the change and cross the same general area in both directions without forcing the endpoints.

### Hear the distinction

In the pitch studio, slide slowly on a quiet “ng” from a comfortable middle note up through the place where the sound starts to change, then back down. Do it twice: once at your usual volume, once at about half that. Volume is the only thing that changes, and the quieter pass usually lets you hear the change coming as a gradual thinning instead of a sudden flip. Note the first note where effort rises on the way up and where the sound settles on the way down: that is a zone, not a single magic pitch. Then compare it with the published zone for your estimated voice type on [vocal range by voice type](/atlas/vocal-range-by-voice-type); if you are a contralto, mezzo or soprano, compare it first with the earlier shift out of chest voice, around E4 to G4, that the same page names.

- Look: Keep the supplied reference or reading visible, settle your posture, and use only a pitch and duration you can repeat comfortably.
- Listen: You can anticipate the change and cross the same general area in both directions without forcing the endpoints.

- Look: Note the pitch, key, or setup you used so that your next comparison is useful.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If no transition is clear, reduce volume, change vowel, or work with a qualified voice teacher. Do not manufacture one by pressing.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.
