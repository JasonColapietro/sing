## In short

These are four production choices you can make on a note, separate from how high it is. Neutral is your easy, speech-like default; restrained is the same note held back, softer and more contained; driven is fuller, like calling across a room; edged is brighter and more cutting, still not loud. Neutral and restrained work across your range; driven and edged get harder higher up, so try them low first and leave edged until a teacher has heard it.

### Set a safe baseline

Use one mid-range phrase at moderate volume and establish a neutral baseline. Use neutral and restrained, add driven only if both are easy, and leave edged out until a teacher has heard it. Neutral: your easy, speech-like default. Restrained: the same note held back, softer and more contained. Driven: fuller and more speech-like, like calling across a room at a moderate level. Edged: brighter and more cutting, still not loud.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Weight and mode switching

Four production choices that are separate from pitch height. Neutral and restrained work across your range; driven and edged get harder the higher you go, so try them low in your range first. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: The same phrase, key, and tempo are used; the jaw, neck, and chin remain available in every version.
- Listen: Each version you sang is audible as a different weight at the same pitch, without persistent rasp, pressure, or loss of range afterward.

### Hear the distinction

Take one short mid-range phrase and sing it twice: neutral, then restrained. Keep the pitch, words and tempo the same and change only the weight, not just the volume. Record both in one take in the recorder with a breath between them. If both were easy, add driven as a third version low in your range, and stop the moment it tightens. Change vocal weight without moving the pitch or using loudness as the only cue. Return to neutral between every attempt.

- Look: The same phrase, key, and tempo are used; the jaw, neck, and chin remain available in every version.
- Listen: Each version you sang is audible as a different weight at the same pitch, without persistent rasp, pressure, or loss of range afterward.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If a heavier mode requires pressure, reduce the contrast or stop. Do not repeat a tight attempt in pursuit of a label.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.
