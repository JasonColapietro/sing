## In short

Water reaches your voice slowly: steady drinking across the day helps; a glass just before singing does little. Dry air counts too, so the NIDCD's Taking Care of Your Voice guidance suggests a humidifier at home. It also says to avoid whispering and talking in noisy places, because both strain the voice. The Measured Voice, chapter 6, adds that most voice fatigue comes from talking. Rest with silence, not a whisper, and count a loud evening out as voice use.

### Set a safe baseline

Check how the voice feels before making sound. Choose only drills that have previously felt easy, and plan pauses and water breaks.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Vocal health

Whispering is on the harmful list. So is talking over noise. Neither is obvious. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: Keep the supplied reference or reading visible, settle your posture, and use only a pitch and duration you can repeat comfortably.
- Listen: The voice feels and sounds at least as easy after the routine as before it. Persistent hoarseness or pain is a reason to stop, not warm up harder.

### Plan the day for your voice

Write down tomorrow's voice use, not just your practice: where you will talk, for how long, and whether there will be noise to talk over. Next to each noisy stretch, write one change, such as moving the conversation somewhere quieter, keeping it short, or using a microphone if you have to address a group. Plan water across the day rather than a glass just before singing, note whether the air where you sleep is dry, and if you need to rest your voice, plan silence rather than whispering. Then build a short warm-up from First hum, Humming thirds and Descending five, record it in the recorder, and stop before fatigue changes the sound.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If hoarseness, discomfort or a changed speaking voice persists, reduce voice use and see a doctor who can examine the vocal folds, such as an ENT or laryngologist. The app and this checklist do not diagnose or clear a voice for continued use.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.
