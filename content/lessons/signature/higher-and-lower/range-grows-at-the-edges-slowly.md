## In short

The highest and lowest notes you can make are set largely by your anatomy, and practice does not redesign anatomy; what it changes is your access to notes that were already there but badly coordinated. The edges follow the middle and rarely lead it, which is why range work comes this late, once the middle is reliable. Add one semitone at a time, count it only when it is comfortable on three separate days, and compare readings over months rather than sessions.

## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose Small three-note climb or Chromatic neighbor. Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading Tracking change over months, not days is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Synthesized pitches supply the notes; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Warm up in the comfortable middle, then confirm today's repeatable low and high notes in the [pitch studio](/studio), which names each note as you sing it, and write them down before approaching either edge.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Range extension

Why this is Stage 7 and not Stage 1, despite being the reason most people download a singing app. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: The added note is reached without a lifted chin, collapsed posture, or a different microphone setup that could distort the comparison.
- Listen: The new endpoint sounds like a usable sung note and can be repeated, not a breathy touch or forced shout reached once.

### Hear the distinction

After warming up in the middle, sing Small three-note climb with its top note on your current comfortable high note, then once more a semitone higher, both at low-to-moderate volume. Change only the key. Record both in one take in the recorder with a breath between them, and on playback ask whether the higher top note is a usable sung note you could repeat or a one-off reach. Count the new note only if it is easy on three separate days, and leave the range test for your next retest, weeks apart, after the same warm-up as your baseline.

- Look: The added note is reached without a lifted chin, collapsed posture, or a different microphone setup that could distort the comparison.
- Listen: The new endpoint sounds like a usable sung note and can be repeated, not a breathy touch or forced shout reached once.

### Review your own attempt

I compared comfortable notes with an earlier baseline and recorded my observation without forcing an extension. Compare your attempt with the supplied material and choose one smaller adjustment for next time.

- Look: Note the pitch, key, or setup you used so that your next comparison is useful.
- Listen: Listen for the specific change taught in this lesson. A self-check is your own reflection, not an automatic vocal score.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If the semitone is not easy today, keep the previous endpoint. Stop for discomfort or persistent voice change and do not count an extreme as progress.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.

## Practice blocks

- 45s: Open the practice material. Prepare the room, body, and safe baseline for “Range Grows at the Edges, Slowly.”
- 75s: Isolate the range extension target in short, comfortable examples.
- 120s: Practice or record the complete “Range Grows at the Edges, Slowly” task, resetting between attempts.
- 60s: Review one take against the stated listening goal and write the next smaller correction.

What this shows: I compared comfortable notes with an earlier baseline and recorded my observation without forcing an extension. This is a self-report, not an automatic assessment.

What this does not show: An automatic pitch, rhythm, register, tone, or safety result. Permanent range growth, vocal health, absence of strain, passaggio movement, or readiness to sing repertoire at the new edge.
