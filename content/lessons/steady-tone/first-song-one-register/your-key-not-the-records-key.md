## In short

A recording's key was chosen for one singer on one set of days: a fact about that session, not the song. Move the whole song by semitones until the stretch where the melody spends most of its time sits in the easy middle of your voice, then check that the top comes without a push. After a range test, songs open fitted to your range; without one, they open in the written key and are scored in either octave.

## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose Amazing Grace or Amazing Grace (Opening Lines, Verses 1 & 2). Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading Choosing songs that actually fit is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Piano notes supply the pitches; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Start from the key the song opens in: fitted to your measured range if you have taken the range test; if not, the written key, with either octave scored. Shift it by semitones until every phrase fits the easy part of your voice. Speak the words and mark breaths before singing the supplied study.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: First repertoire

The song moves to you. This is the singing equivalent of a capo. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: The chosen key keeps the melody inside your range; phrase starts, breaths, and the final note are marked before the take.
- Listen: The pitch center and words stay recognizable from beginning to end, without a forced high note or a fading low note.

### Hear the distinction

Open Amazing Grace in the songbook and sing its opening phrase (“Amazing grace, how sweet the sound, that saved a wretch like me”) in the key it opens in. Then shift it one semitone up or down with the transpose control, whichever way looks easier, and sing the same phrase again. The key is the only thing that changes. Compare the second line of each: keep the key in which the highest note comes without a push and the lowest is still clearly audible, and write it down. Keep one consistent, comfortable production through the phrase.

- Look: The chosen key keeps the melody inside your range; phrase starts, breaths, and the final note are marked before the take.
- Listen: The pitch center and words stay recognizable from beginning to end, without a forced high note or a fading low note.

### Review your own attempt

I sang the opening phrase of Amazing Grace in its opening key and again a semitone up or down, and wrote down the key where the top came without a push and the bottom stayed audible. Compare your attempt with the supplied material and choose one smaller adjustment for next time.

- Look: Note the pitch, key, or setup you used so that your next comparison is useful.
- Listen: Listen for the specific change taught in this lesson. A self-check is your own reflection, not an automatic vocal score.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If one phrase repeatedly tightens or disappears, transpose again or shorten the phrase. A famous key is never more important than a usable one.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.

## Practice blocks

- 30s: Open the practice material. Prepare the room, body, and safe baseline for “Your Key, Not the Record's Key.”
- 45s: Isolate the first repertoire target in short, comfortable examples.
- 60s: Practice or record the complete “Your Key, Not the Record's Key” task, resetting between attempts.
- 45s: Review one take against the stated listening goal and write the next smaller correction.
