## In short

An onset is how a note starts, and there are three basic ways. A breathy onset lets air through before the folds meet, so a small “h” comes first; a hard, or glottal, onset closes the folds before the air arrives and releases them with a click; a balanced onset starts breath and sound together. This module trains the balanced one, on its pitch from the first moment; sliding up from below is a scoop, and onset work fixes it.

## Steps

### Use the practice material

Open the practice material on this page and choose Five-note scale or Chromatic neighbor. Hear the reference, choose a comfortable key, then stop it before singing. The reading What pitch accuracy actually measures is listed there too.

- Look: The exercise, song or chapter is open. Your chosen range remains comfortable.
- Listen: Piano notes supply the pitches; they are not recordings of a singer demonstrating a technique.

### Set a safe baseline

Choose a target near the middle of your comfortable range. Hear it once, stop the reference, breathe normally, and prepare the vowel before starting.

- Look: Posture, room, device, and the planned stop rule are settled before the first attempt.
- Listen: Begin from an easy, repeatable sound; silence playback before judging your own voice.

### Name the target: Onset and pitch matching

Compare a breathy, a clicked and a balanced onset on one note; the piano reference supplies pitch, not a demonstration of vocal technique. Say what will count as complete in this lesson before practicing, and keep the attempt inside the range and duration you can repeat comfortably.

- Look: Keep the supplied reference or reading visible, settle your posture, and use only a pitch and duration you can repeat comfortably.
- Listen: The beginning sounds clean and intentional, with one pitch center rather than a click, breathy delay, or rising slide.

### Hear the distinction

Sing “ah” on one comfortable note three ways in one take, with a quiet breath between each: first with a soft “h” leading in (breathy); then once, gently, from a held breath so it starts with a small catch (the click); then with breath and sound starting together, like the start of a sigh without the “h”. Only the onset changes, so keep the pitch and the volume the same. The third, on its pitch from the first moment rather than sliding up from below, is the onset this module trains. Record your takes in the recorder. Hold briefly, release, and compare a fresh attempt instead of steering one long note with your eyes.

- Look: Keep the supplied reference or reading visible, settle your posture, and use only a pitch and duration you can repeat comfortably.
- Listen: The beginning sounds clean and intentional, with one pitch center rather than a click, breathy delay, or rising slide.

## If it goes wrong

- The target disappears when the range, volume, speed, or duration increases. => If you scoop, silently imagine the pitch, use a quiet hum first, then open to the vowel without changing pitch. Reduce volume if the onset feels hard.
- The reference and your own sound seem different, or you cannot judge the result. => Treat the reading as missing, not as a pass or failure. Stop reference audio, restore a quiet setup, make one easier fresh attempt, and keep the human listening judgment separate from the measurement.
